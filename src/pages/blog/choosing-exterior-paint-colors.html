How to choose exterior paint colors for your Kansas City home

By Cade Zerr · August 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Picking exterior colors is the fun part of a repaint, and also the part homeowners worry about most. A color that looks perfect on a small chip can look completely different across an entire house in Kansas City sun. Here's the process we use with our clients.

Start with what isn't changing

Your roof, brick, stone, and windows aren't getting repainted, so your colors need to work with them. Look at the undertones:

  • Warm roofs and brick (browns, reds, tans) pair best with warm whites, creams, greiges, and earthy greens.
  • Cool roofs (gray, black, charcoal) open the door to cool grays, crisp whites, navy, and charcoal.
  • White or almond vinyl windows limit how dark you can go on trim without clashing. Plan around them.

Think in three colors

  1. Body: the main siding color and the biggest visual impact
  2. Trim: window and door casings, fascia, and corner boards, usually a contrasting white or a darker tone
  3. Accent: front door, shutters, or garage doors, where you can be bolder

Popular directions we're painting in Kansas City

  • Charcoal and near-black bodies with white trim for a modern, high-contrast look
  • Warm greiges and soft whites that feel updated without being stark
  • Cool grays with bright white trim, a timeless option on two-story homes
  • Deep greens and blues as accents on doors and shutters

Always test samples on your house

Color shifts dramatically with light. A gray that looks neutral indoors can turn blue in shade or wash out in full sun. We recommend:

  • Painting large samples (at least 2 feet square) on more than one side of the house
  • Looking at them in morning, midday, and evening light
  • Viewing them from the street, not just up close

Tip: exterior colors usually look lighter and brighter on the house than on the chip. If you're torn between two shades, the slightly darker one is often closer to what you're picturing.

Check your HOA first

Many Johnson County and Lee's Summit neighborhoods have HOA color guidelines or require approval before a color change. We can provide paint names, color codes, and samples for your submission.
