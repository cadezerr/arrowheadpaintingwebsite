Does your house actually need to be repainted? 7 signs to check

By Cade Zerr · September 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Not every faded-looking house needs a full repaint right away. Sometimes the paint is still in good shape, and a little maintenance buys you several more years. Other times, what looks cosmetic is actually water getting into your siding.

Walk around your house with this checklist. Start with the south and west sides, which take the most sun and usually wear first.

1. Peeling, flaking, or blistering paint

This is the clearest sign. When paint lifts, it no longer protects the wood underneath. Small isolated spots can be scraped and touched up, but peeling across large areas means it's time for a repaint.

2. Chalking

Rub your hand across the siding. If it comes away with a powdery residue, the paint's binder is breaking down. Chalky paint has lost much of its protection and won't hold a fresh coat properly unless it's washed and prepped.

3. Cracked or missing caulk

Look at the joints around windows, doors, corner boards, and where siding meets trim. Failed caulk is how water gets behind your siding, and it's often the first step toward rot. If caulk is the main problem, re-caulking can be a smart short-term fix.

4. Bare or graying wood

Any wood without paint is absorbing moisture. Gray, weathered wood on trim or siding needs to be prepped and primed soon.

5. Soft spots or wood rot

Press on the bottoms of door frames, window sills, and trim boards near the ground. Soft or spongy wood means rot has already started. Here's how to spot and repair it.

6. Fading that's uneven

Some fading is normal. But if the sunny sides of your house are noticeably lighter than the shady sides, the coating is wearing thin where it matters most.

7. It's been 8 to 12 years

Most Kansas City exteriors need a repaint every 7 to 12 years, depending on siding material, sun exposure, paint quality, and how well the last job was prepped. If you don't know when the house was last painted, that's a good reason to have it checked.

When maintenance is enough

If your paint is still tight and colorful, and the issues are limited to a few caulk joints or a small area of peeling, a touch-up and re-caulk can protect your home for a few more years. We'll tell you honestly if that's the case. A full repaint isn't always the right answer.

Selling soon? A fresh exterior is one of the most visible upgrades buyers notice, and fixing peeling paint and rot ahead of time can head off inspection issues.
