Can you paint a house exterior in winter in Kansas City?

By Cade Zerr · October 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick Answer

Sometimes. Several modern exterior paints, including Sherwin-Williams SuperPaint, are rated to go on as low as 35°F. The catch is that the air and the siding have to stay above that through the afternoon, the surface has to be dry, and the paint needs a few hours before the evening dew or a freeze. In Kansas City that gives you a handful of good days most winters, not a reliable schedule.

Every December somebody calls us with the same question. Maybe they just closed on a house, or the HOA sent a letter, or a rotted trim board finally gave up. They want to know if exterior painting has to wait until spring.

Not always. But winter painting in Kansas City is a weather call that gets made one day at a time.

What temperature does exterior paint need?

Older exterior paints, and many budget lines today, need the air and surface to be at least 50°F. Several premium paints are now rated for application down to 35°F. Sherwin-Williams SuperPaint is one of them, and it's also the product behind our 3-year exterior warranty.

That number on the can is a floor, not a target. Three other things matter just as much:

  • Surface temperature. North walls and shaded siding can sit several degrees colder than the air. We check the wall, not the weather app.
  • Moisture. Frost, melting snow, and morning dew all have to burn off before paint goes on. Wet wood won't hold paint in any season.
  • The evening. Paint needs a few dry hours to set before the temperature drops toward the dew point. A 45°F afternoon that turns into a 20°F night with heavy dew is a bad painting day.

What a Kansas City winter actually looks like

Kansas City winters swing hard. January highs average around 40°F, but we'll get a 60°F week followed by a stretch below zero. That's why we don't promise winter exterior dates weeks in advance. We watch the 10-day forecast and jump on the windows that work.

A good winter painting day here usually looks like this: a high in the upper 40s or warmer, sun on the side of the house we're painting, no rain or snow on the ground, and a low that doesn't drop hard until well after dark. Crews start later, once frost is off, and wrap up early.

Our honest take: winter is fine for small exterior jobs like a front door, a section of new trim, or primer on fresh wood repairs. A full repaint of a whole house is better scheduled for late spring through fall, when we can paint every side in good conditions.

What works well in winter

  • Wood rot repair. Rotted boards can be replaced in almost any weather, then primed on a warm day and finish-painted later. Waiting until spring just gives water more time to spread. See our wood rot repair.
  • Front doors and small areas on a sunny side of the house.
  • Interior painting. This is the easy answer. Indoor conditions are stable, and winter is the least busy time of year for interior projects.
  • Getting on the spring schedule. Spring and fall book up fast in Kansas City. Winter is the best time to get your estimate done and your colors picked so you're first in line when it warms up.

When to wait

Wait if the house has a lot of peeling paint that needs scraping and priming on every side, if you're making a big color change, or if the project needs several days of steady weather in a row. Rushing a full exterior into a cold week to save a couple of months is how you end up repainting sooner than you should.
