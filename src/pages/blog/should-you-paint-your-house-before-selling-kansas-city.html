Should you paint your house before selling?

By Cade Zerr · October 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick Answer

Usually, yes, but you don't always need to paint everything. Fix peeling exterior paint and any wood rot first, since buyers notice it and inspectors write it up. Then repaint rooms with bold, dated, or scuffed walls in a light neutral. Leave anything that already looks clean and fresh alone.

If you're about to sell, you've probably heard "paint before you list" from your agent, your neighbor, and half the internet. It's good advice. But it's not a blank check to repaint the whole house.

Here's how we'd think about it if it were our own house going on the market.

Why painting before selling helps

Real estate agents recommend fresh paint before listing more than almost any other project, and for a simple reason: it changes how a house looks in photos and in person for a small fraction of what a remodel costs. Buyers scroll through listings fast. Clean walls, crisp trim, and a front door that pops all help a house stand out.

Paint also takes questions off the table. Peeling paint and soft trim make buyers wonder what else hasn't been kept up, and they're the kind of items an inspector writes up.

What to paint first

  1. Anything peeling or rotted outside. Fix the wood, then paint. This is the one area we'd never skip. Here's where rot usually hides.
  2. The front door and front trim. It's the first thing in every listing photo and the first thing buyers touch.
  3. Bold or very personal rooms. A navy dining room or a bright kids' room can look great to you and make it harder for buyers to picture themselves there.
  4. Scuffed, high-traffic areas. Hallways, stairways, and entries show wear first.
  5. Trim and doors that have yellowed or chipped.

Rooms that already look clean in a neutral color can usually be left alone. A touch-up is enough.

Colors that help a house sell

Go light and warm inside: soft whites, warm greiges, and light grays. They photograph well, make rooms feel bigger, and work with almost any buyer's furniture. Outside, stay with combinations that fit your street. A white or light body with dark trim, or a mid-tone gray or greige with white trim, are safe bets on most Kansas City homes. More on picking exterior colors.

Older home? If your house was built before 1978, any paint that gets disturbed may contain lead. Federal rules require paid contractors to follow lead-safe work practices on those homes, so ask about it when you get estimates.

Timing it with your listing date

Work backward from the day the photographer shows up. Get estimates about four to six weeks out, longer in spring and fall when schedules fill fast. Interiors usually take a few days. Exteriors take a few days to a week, and rain can push that. Give yourself a buffer so you're not staging furniture in a room that's still wet.
