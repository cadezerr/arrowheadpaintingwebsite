The best time of year to paint your house exterior in Kansas City

By Cade Zerr · September 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Kansas City gives us four real seasons, and every one of them affects how exterior paint goes on and how long it lasts. Timing your project right is one of the easiest ways to get a better result, so here's how we think about the calendar.

The short answer

Late spring through fall is the exterior painting season in Kansas City, roughly April through early November depending on the year. Within that window, fall is often the best time of all: steady, moderate temperatures, lower humidity, and fewer pop-up storms than spring.

What paint actually needs

Exterior paint doesn't just dry. It cures, and that process depends on conditions for several hours after it's applied. Here's what matters most:

  • Temperature. Many of today's premium exterior paints can be applied in cooler weather than older products, but both the air and the surface need to stay above the product's minimum while the paint dries. Overnight lows matter as much as the afternoon high.
  • Dew and humidity. If a surface is damp from morning dew or cools below the dew point in the evening, paint can stay soft, streak, or lose adhesion. That's why we start after surfaces dry off and stop early enough for the paint to set before evening.
  • Direct sun and heat. On hot summer days, siding in direct sun can get much hotter than the air. Paint dries too fast, shows lap marks, and doesn't level properly. We follow the shade around the house in July and August.
  • Rain. Fresh paint needs a few dry hours before rain. We watch the radar closely and won't start a side of the house if a storm is coming.

Season by season in Kansas City

Spring (April–May)

Spring is a busy, productive time, but the weather is unpredictable. Wet weeks and severe storms can push schedules around, and cool nights limit how late in the day we can paint. Spring is a great time to get your estimate and lock in a date.

Summer (June–August)

Long, dry days are good for production, but heat and humidity require planning. Our crews start early, work the shady sides in the heat of the day, and avoid painting dark colors in full afternoon sun.

Fall (September–early November)

This is the sweet spot. Cooler temperatures, lower humidity, and more stable weather give coatings excellent conditions to cure. It's also the last chance to fix peeling paint, failed caulk, and exposed wood before freeze-thaw cycles start working water into every crack.

Plan ahead: fall is our busiest season, and the schedule fills quickly. If you're thinking about a fall repaint, reach out in late summer.

Winter (December–March)

Exterior painting in a Kansas City winter is rarely a good idea. Cold surfaces and short days don't leave enough time for paint to cure. Winter is a great time for interior projects and for getting on the schedule for spring.

Don't wait if you see these

The calendar matters, but your house's condition matters more. If you see peeling paint, bare wood, cracked caulk, or soft trim, water is already getting in. Waiting another season usually means more wood rot repair later. Read 7 signs your house needs to be repainted for a quick checklist.
